How to play and controls

The dinosaur game is an endless runner. A small T-Rex stands in a grey desert. When you start, the ground begins to scroll and cacti come in from the right. Jump them, and later duck or jump the pterodactyls, for as long as you can. There is no finish line. A run ends only when you hit something. The number in the top right is distance, not a combo, and the high score on this page is stored on this device only.

On a computer, press Space or the Up arrow to start and to jump. A short tap gives a short jump, which is enough for a single small cactus. Hold the key to jump higher and clear a tall cluster. Press the Down arrow to duck. If you press Down in mid-air, the dinosaur drops faster, which is useful when a gap is tight. After a crash, Enter restarts immediately; Space or Up wait a short moment first. You can also click the restart icon on the Game Over panel.

On a phone or tablet, tap anywhere on the game to start and to jump. Use the Down arrow to duck when a keyboard is available. Rotate to landscape if you want a wider view of the approaching obstacles. The game is designed to start instantly: there is no account wall and no pre-roll. Press Mute if you want the beeps off, or Fullscreen to give the runner the whole screen.

You can also open the original in the Chrome browser at chrome://dino, or from Chrome’s “No internet” page. This site is an unofficial online copy so you can play with the internet on, on any modern browser.

Tips for a higher score

Watch the right-hand side of the desert, not the dinosaur. Obstacles are easier to judge when you see them arriving than when you stare at the T-Rex’s feet. Use a short tap for a small cactus and hold for a tall cluster. Press Down in mid-air to drop faster and get ready for the next one.

Pterodactyls appear after a few hundred points, at three heights. Jump the low ones, duck the mid ones, and run under the high ones. Night mode inverts the colours around every 700 points for about 12 seconds; the obstacles themselves do not change, so do not let the flash break your timing. Speed caps after the first couple of minutes. At top speed, late jumps cause most crashes, not early ones. The score is simply distance. Your high score stays on this device until a public board exists.

Where the game comes from

The T-Rex runner first appeared on Chrome’s offline error page in September 2014. It was built by the Chrome UX team — Sebastien Gabriel, Alan Bettes and Edward Jung — under the codename Project Bolan, a nod to Marc Bolan of the band T. Rex. The idea was simple: if you have no internet, you still get a tiny game. The pixel dinosaur, the cacti, the pterodactyls and the beep sounds all live in the Chromium project, which is released under the BSD 3-Clause licence.

This site ports the current Chromium dino_game so the look, physics, night mode, score digits and sounds match today’s runner. It is an unofficial fan site. Frequently asked questions

What is the dinosaur game called?

People call it the Dinosaur Game, the Dino Game, the T-Rex Runner, or Chrome’s offline or “no internet” game. The character is sometimes called the Lonely T-Rex. On this site we use Dinosaur Game as the name of the page, and T-Rex runner when we describe what you are playing.

How do you play the T-Rex or dinosaur game?

Press Space or the Up arrow to jump, and the Down arrow to duck. On a phone, tap anywhere on the game to jump. The aim is to avoid cacti and pterodactyls for as long as you can. The run is endless: it only stops when you hit an obstacle.

How do I access the dino game in Chrome? What is the Chrome address?

In the Chrome browser, type chrome://dino into the address bar, or wait for the “No internet” page and start the runner there. This site is an unofficial online copy. You do not need to go offline to play here, and this page is not an official Google or Chrome product.

Is the dino game endless? Can it be beaten?

Yes, it is an endless runner. A run ends only when you hit an obstacle. There is no official finish line. The old joke that it lasts “17 million years” is a nod to the T-Rex era, not a real ending. You cannot “beat” the game in the usual sense; you can only beat your high score.

What happens after 99,999?

In current Chrome code the counter grows to six digits and continues; it does not stop the run. Reaching around 99,999 takes roughly 90 minutes of clean play. There is no official world record, because bots and cheats are easy, so treat huge scores you see online with caution.

Can I play it on my phone?

Yes. Open this page in a mobile browser and tap anywhere on the game to jump. Landscape mode gives you more time to see obstacles. Your high score is saved in this browser on this device.

Is there source code for the Chrome dinosaur game?

Yes. The runner is part of the Chromium project, under the BSD 3-Clause licence. The current TypeScript modules live in components/neterror/resources/dino_game/ in the Chromium source tree. This site’s port and the full licence text are on the licence page.

Who made it, and when?

It was made by Chrome UX designers and engineers — Sebastien Gabriel, Alan Bettes and Edward Jung — and shipped in September 2014. The internal codename was Project Bolan, after Marc Bolan of T. Rex. The code and art are maintained in Chromium by The Chromium Authors.

Can I play with the internet on?

Yes. You can play here with a normal internet connection, or you can open chrome://dino in Chrome even when you are online. You do not need to turn the network off to see the dinosaur.

How do you duck?

On a keyboard, press the Down arrow. If you press Down in mid-air, the dinosaur drops faster. Touch screens jump on a tap; ducking uses the Down arrow when a keyboard is connected.

Why does a Chromebook say “The owner of this device turned off the dinosaur game”?

Some managed Chromebooks hide the easter egg with an admin policy called AllowDinosaurEasterEgg. That only affects the copy inside Chrome on that device. This unofficial site still works in a normal browser.
