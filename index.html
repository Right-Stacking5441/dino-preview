How to play

It’s an endless runner. The T-Rex stands in a grey desert, and once you start, the ground scrolls and cacti come in from the right, with pterodactyls joining them later. The run ends when you hit something. The number in the top right is how far you’ve run.

On a computer, press Space or the Up arrow (or click the game) to start and to jump. Hold the key for a full-height jump over a tall cluster, or let go just after take-off to cut the jump short, which is enough for a single small cactus. Press the Down arrow to duck, or press it in mid-air to drop faster. After a crash, Enter starts a new run straight away. Space and Up work too after a short pause, or you can click the game.

On a phone or tablet, tap the game to start and to jump. You can’t duck by touch, so ducking needs a keyboard. Turn the screen to landscape for a wider view of what’s coming.

There’s nothing to sign up for and no advert before the game. Mute turns the beeps off and Fullscreen gives the runner the whole screen. Your high score and your mute setting are saved in this browser.

Tips for a higher score

Watch the right-hand side of the desert, not the dinosaur. Obstacles are easier to judge when you see them arriving than when you stare at the T-Rex’s feet. At top speed, late jumps cause most crashes, not early ones.

Pterodactyls turn up after a few hundred points. On a computer they fly at three heights: jump the low ones, duck the middle ones and run under the high ones. On phones and tablets there are only two, low and high, so you never have to duck: jump the low ones and run under the high ones. Every 700 points or so, night mode flips the colours for about 12 seconds. The obstacles don’t change, so don’t let the flash throw your timing. Speed tops out within one to two minutes, sooner on a high-refresh-rate screen. Your high score stays on this device.

Where the game comes from

The T-Rex runner first appeared on Chrome’s offline error page in September 2014. It was built by three people on the Chrome UX team, Sebastien Gabriel, Alan Bettes and Edward Jung, under the codename Project Bolan, after Marc Bolan of the band T. Rex. If your connection dropped, Chrome gave you a tiny game instead of a dead end.

This site runs a port of the current Chromium code, so the look, physics, night mode, score digits and sounds match the runner in Chrome today. The main visible difference is that night mode only darkens the game, not the whole page. The licence page has the full notice.

Frequently asked questions

What is the dinosaur game called?

It goes by a few names: the dinosaur game, the dino game, the Chrome dino, the T-Rex runner, or Chrome’s offline or “No internet” game. The character is sometimes called the Lonely T-Rex.

How do I open it in Chrome?

Type chrome://dino into Chrome’s address bar, or wait for the “No internet” page and start the game there. The address works even when you’re online.

Does it ever end?

No. There’s no finish line, and a run only ends when you hit something. The “17 million years” you may have heard about is a nod to how long the T-Rex was around, not a real ending. The only thing to beat is your own high score.

What happens after 99,999?

The counter goes to six digits and the run carries on. By our estimate from Chrome’s code, reaching 99,999 takes roughly 90 minutes of clean play.

Is there an official world record?

Not that we know of. Google doesn’t publish a leaderboard or an official record, and bots and cheats are easy, so treat huge scores you see online with caution.

Why does a Chromebook say “The owner of this device turned off the dinosaur game”?

Some managed Chromebooks hide the game with an admin policy called AllowDinosaurEasterEgg. That only affects Chrome’s own copy on that device. This site still works in a normal browser.

Is there source code for the Chrome dinosaur game?

Yes. The runner is part of the Chromium project, under the BSD 3-Clause licence. The current TypeScript code lives in components/neterror/resources/dino_game/ in the Chromium source tree. Our port and the full licence text are on the licence page.
